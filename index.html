The basics

What is an Export Purpose Code?

An Export Purpose Code is a five-character code — always starting with P — that the Reserve Bank of India uses to classify every cross-border foreign-currency receipt into a service or goods category.

When an overseas client pays you in USD, EUR, or GBP, your Authorised Dealer (AD) bank must report that receipt to the RBI's balance-of-payments system. The code tells the RBI what you were paid for. Without it, the bank can't issue your FIRA / e-BRC — which means the receipt can't be reconciled against your GST exports.

  • Mandated by RBI under the FED Master Direction on Export of Goods & Services.
  • Roughly 300 codes exist in the live Annexure. Most SMBs only ever touch two or three.
  • Filed by your AD bank, but you're the one who has to pick it (or argue with them when they pick the wrong one).
  • Not the same as a HS Code (goods customs) or SAC Code (GST). Each has a different taxonomy and purpose.
